in the Super Intelligence Era

Don't trust the model. Build a system where you don't have to.

You can't read a frontier model like a stack trace.

With old software, any behavior could be traced back to a line of code someone wrote and could explain. Frontier models don't work that way — their internal reasoning is largely opaque even to the people who trained them. And yet we hand these systems our sensitive data and let them take actions on our behalf. The only sane response is to treat every model like an insider risk: a powerful actor you can't fully audit from the inside, so you put the controls outside the model instead of trusting what's inside it.

Seven principles for observable, containable AI

01

Model diversity

No model is the sole dependency, and no model verifies its own work.

02

Observe everything

Every action leaves tamper-proof, human-readable evidence. If it can't be observed, it can't be trusted.

03

Verifiability

Continuously test the whole system — including its failures and the attacks against it.

04

Independent controls

Organizations independently decide what a model can access and what it's allowed to do.

05

Independent auditability

The validator can't be the model.

06

Containment

Assume compromise. An authorized human can pause or shut it down mid-task.

07

Incident disclosure

Disclose failures quickly and share learnings industrywide.

"The most trustworthy system won't be the one with the model we trust most. It will be the one that lets us trust the model the least."
